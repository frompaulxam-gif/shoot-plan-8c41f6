## 3. The categories, and why they group

The grouping is not by theme. It is by **what the shot physically needs**, because that is what decides whether two things can happen in the same hour.

| Cat | Needs | Crowd? | Camera | Post work |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Drinks in the sun** | direct sun, one table, drinks made to order | no, must be empty | one locked tripod, does not move | medium (the appear-cuts and the phone UI) |
| **B. Empty to full** | the same day, twice, hours apart. **three pairs**, and one needs height | both states | tripod on three taped marks | low |
| **C. Room at peak** | a real busy room | yes, the busier the better | handheld plus tripod | medium (split screen, labels) |
| **D. Entrance overhead** | a high mount point over the door | yes, arrivals | clamped, unattended | low plus one end card |
| **E. AI and CCTV** | an empty locked-off plate | no | tripod, 15 min total | high, all at the desk |
| **F. Staff and bar** | staff time and the bar | no | one locked tripod | low |
| **G. People** | face level, inside the group, and consent | yes, and the two long ones need a **big** night | handheld, close | low to medium |

Read down the "needs" column and the day builds itself. A, E and F all want an empty venue and a static camera. B wants the empty state and then the full state. C and D want the crowd. So: empty stuff in the morning, crowd stuff after doors.

## 6. Two honest flags on the AI idea

**Rights.** Spider-Man is a Marvel and Disney trademark. Michael Jackson's likeness is controlled by his estate. Jude Bellingham is a living person with active commercial deals and image rights. Putting any of them in paid promotional content for a client is real exposure, not a technicality, and it is the client who carries it. The Alcove reel works because a big cat is not owned by anybody. Keep the mechanic and swap the subject: a leopard, a gorilla, an escaped penguin, an astronaut, a knight in armour, a Yeti for the winter season. Same joke, no letter from a lawyer. If a celebrity angle is genuinely wanted, the safe route is a real lookalike or an obvious parody framing, agreed with the venue in writing first.

**Realism.** The Alcove reel is convincing because CCTV is low resolution, high angle, greyish and grainy. That framing hides every AI tell. The same creature composited into a clean, sharp daylight hero shot will read plastic instantly and will make the venue look cheap, which is the opposite of the brief. So keep all AI content inside "found footage" framings: security cam, a phone held in a crowd, night, motion blur, someone's story repost. Grade the AI element to match the plate, add matching grain, and check it at 100 percent before it goes out.

## 7. Kit and post notes

- **Do not move the tripod** during block A. Every appear-cut in that block depends on it. Tape the legs, lock the head, note the focal length and exposure.
- Props to bring: a **second phone**, a **physical menu** and **a pair of glasses** plus someone willing to wear them. All three are in shot.
- The empty-to-full reel is **three pairs, not one**, so it needs three sets of taped marks and the overhead height solved before doors.
- Shoot the sunlit block at **60fps** so you have the option of slow motion. The Oast House and Eat & Go references are both 60fps sources.
- **Aspect ratios differ by format.** The split screens and the CCTV reel are 4:3. Everything else is 9:16. Frame with headroom so one take crops to both.
- The brightness-slider reel needs **two exposures of the identical frame**, flat and bright. Shoot both back to back, do not try to fix it in the grade.
- The overhead entrance rig needs the venue's permission for the mount and a **filming notice** at the door. Sort that before the day, not on it.
- On-screen text is doing real work in six of these fourteen. Brew ffmpeg here has no libass, so burn text with the Pillow plus overlay pipeline in `~/Downloads/film_b_subtitled/tools/`.

## 8. Tone check against the brand guidelines

The guidelines call for fun, chatty, welcoming, slightly cheeky, never try-hard, and warn that "if it feels too niche, we've gone wrong". Worth noting a real tension: the earlier content plan pushed the 30 to 45 crowd and the England v Panama plan pushed 35 plus, while the guidelines say 18 plus with broad appeal. Resolve it in the **casting**, not the format. Shoot the after-work and older-group crowd on purpose in block C rather than defaulting to the youngest and loudest table, and the formats stay broad while the audience skew comes out right.

Caption direction that fits the voice, using the actual references:

- Empty to full: "2pm vs 9pm. Same yard."
- Cocktail in the sun: "Sun's out. You know where to be."
- Text overlay: "You said: let's get a drink. I heard: Merchants Yard."
- Split screen: "Why people come. Why people stay."
- Overhead wave: "POV: you pulled up to Merchants Yard."
- Blind pour: "Put the team to the test. Blindfolded."
- AI CCTV: "Checked the cameras this morning. Full info Thursday."
