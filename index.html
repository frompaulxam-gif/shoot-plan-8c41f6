Shoot plan · rest of season 2026 · built 4 Aug

19 reels. 14 formats. One Saturday.

Every reference below was downloaded and watched frame by frame, not read off the caption. Three turned out to be the same trend twice over. Grouped by what the shot physically needs rather than by theme, seventeen of the nineteen fit into a single day, 11:00 to 23:00.

Six of them need direct sun. It is 4 August and the season closes on 26 September. That block cannot slip. The two long people pieces are the exception to the one-day rule: they want a genuinely big night, so they go on 21 Aug or the bank holiday.

Run of day

Read the “needs” column of the blocks above and the day builds itself. A, F and E all want an empty venue and a static camera, so they go in the morning. C and D want the crowd, so they go after doors. B brackets the whole thing.

  1. 10:45—Load in. Find where the sun actually lands at midday and pick the hero table. Tape three tripod marks now for the empty-to-full pairs: overhead yard wide, the bar, the bench tables. Solve the overhead height while you are here — upstairs window, balcony or ladder. Photograph each tape position and write down the focal length and exposure.
  2. 11:00AClean drinks, best light. 60fps. POV seated with hands in, straw stir, clink, condensation macro, top-down with the logo, wide of the table with the yard behind. Grid stills in the same setup.
  3. 12:15AGimmicks. Camera does not move. Empty table pass, then the same frame with the drink already there. Phone-tap. Menu page turns. Flat pass then bright pass for the brightness reel. The glasses wipe.
  4. 13:00FStaff challenges. Blind Pour with four or five staff. Let them fail. Two or three extra challenge ideas while everyone is already there.
  5. 13:40EPlate bank. Six locked-off empty wides, fifteen seconds each. Entrance from as high as you can get, bar, big screen, stage, full yard, corner seating. Ask for the real CCTV angle.
  6. 13:55BThree empty passes on the taped marks: overhead yard, bar, bench tables.
  7. 14:00DDoors. Mount the overhead rig above the entrance, start it rolling and leave it.
  8. 14:00CFirst groups arriving, drinks going out, the yard filling.
  9. 17:00CGolden hour. Best people footage of the day. Faces, laughing, groups mixing, low sun through glasses.
  10. 17:00GEarly doors, one person one drink. The after-work crowd, not the youngest table. Staff member carries the drink out, hands it over, says the line. This is the 30 to 45 casting note as an actual shot.
  11. 19:00CPeak. DJ or live act, full yard, the screen, the bar working. Wide enough to crop landscape and vertical from one take.
  12. 19:00GGet inside the crowd. Face level, handheld, in the group. Bank the five person looks: staff mid-task, someone being photographed, a group under the signage, backs of the bar queue, the full wide. Be in position before the goal or the drop, not after it.
  13. 21:00BThree full passes on the same marks: yard busy, bar three deep, tables dancing. Shoot the dancing one last — it needs real dancing, so it may not be there until closer to 23:00.
  14. 23:00—Wrap. Pull the overhead rig. Offload cards before you leave the car. Note which formats you actually got.

Read before commissioning any of it

Two flags on the AI idea

Flag 01 · rights

Nobody owns a leopard

Spider-Man is a Marvel and Disney trademark. Michael Jackson’s likeness sits with his estate. Jude Bellingham is a living person with active commercial deals and image rights. In paid promotional content for a venue, that exposure lands on the client, and it is not a technicality.

The Alcove reel works precisely because a big cat belongs to no one. Keep the mechanic and swap the subject: a leopard, a gorilla, an escaped penguin, an astronaut, a knight in armour, a Yeti for the winter season. Same joke, no letter. If a celebrity angle is genuinely wanted, the safe route is a real lookalike or an obvious parody framing, agreed with the venue in writing first.

Flag 02 · realism

Hide it in found footage

The Alcove reel is convincing because CCTV is low resolution, high angle, greyish and grainy. That framing hides every AI tell there is.

The same creature composited into a clean, sharp daylight hero shot will read plastic instantly and make the venue look cheap, which is the opposite of the brief. Keep all AI content inside found-footage framings: security cam, a phone held in a crowd, night, motion blur, someone’s story repost. Grade the AI element to match the plate, add matching grain, and check it at 100 percent before it goes out.

The things that break the shot if you forget them

Kit & post

  • Do not move the tripod during block A. Every appear-cut in that block depends on it. Tape the legs, lock the head, note the focal length and exposure.
  • Shoot the sunlit block at 60fps so slow motion stays an option. Both sun references on the list are 60fps sources.
  • Aspect ratios differ by format. The two split screens and the CCTV reel are landscape. Everything else is vertical. Frame with headroom so one take crops to both.
  • The brightness reel needs two exposures of the identical frame, flat and bright. Shoot both back to back rather than trying to fix it in the grade.
  • Props to bring: a second phone, a physical menu and a pair of glasses plus someone willing to wear them. All three are in shot.
  • The empty-to-full reel is three pairs, not one, so it needs three sets of taped marks and the overhead position solved before doors. The dancing pair is the only shot on the day that cannot be rushed.
  • The overhead rig needs the venue’s permission for the mount and a filming notice at the door.
  • Block G is close-up faces, so a notice at the door is not enough on its own. Get a proper yes from anyone who carries a shot, especially the one-person piece, and be ready to drop a clip if someone changes their mind. Cheaper than an awkward DM later.
  • On-screen text does real work in six of the fourteen. Brew ffmpeg here has no libass, so burn text with the Pillow plus overlay pipeline in ~/Downloads/film_b_subtitled/tools/.
  • The brief pushes 30 to 45 while the brand guidelines say 18 plus with broad appeal. Resolve that in the casting, not the format.

Who to watch

UK venues of the same type: yards, terraces, container parks and social spaces with sport screens. Every handle resolves to a live profile whose page title matches the venue. Follower counts are second-hand and should be treated as ballpark.

  1. 01 @digbethdiningclubBirmingham · ~109K Your closest analogue at scale, and in your region. Steal the vendor-of-the-week format: make the trader the character, not the venue. Endless content without needing a new event every week.
  2. 02 @stjamesstackNewcastle · matchday fan zone Four of your five people references came from here, and it is the closest venue on the list to Merchants Yard. Steal the three-act fixture structure: build-up as the crowd fills, reaction on the big screen, wind-down after.
  3. 03 @balticmarketLiverpool · ~132K Biggest short-form library in the north for this venue type. Steal the matchday cadence and the way they cut food macro against wide crowd shots inside the same fifteen seconds.
  4. 04 @peddlermktSheffield · ~42K A monthly night market turned into a content engine. Steal the countdown loop: announce, line-up reveal, night-of, recap. Maps straight onto a weekly Friday and Saturday cycle.
  5. 05 @binksyardNottingham · ~27K Nearest big-city peer at a scale you can actually resource against. Steal the re-skin: open-air cinema, themed nights, one space presented as several distinct events.
  6. 06 @templeof.funSheffield · ~62K Strongest visual identity here. Steal the art-direction discipline: a locked colour and lighting look, so a clip reads as theirs before the logo appears.
  7. 07 @boxparkcroydonLondon · fan park The reference for crowd energy. Steal the reaction-cam framing: point the camera at the crowd during a goal, not at the screen. Highest-shareability shot you have, and it costs nothing.
  8. 08 @tigeryardcardiffCardiff · ~30K Closest match to your actual problem: a yard that opens for summer. Steal the season-opening hype run and how they sell covered and heated space without killing the outdoor promise.

Three handles worth correcting

  • Lawn Club is @lawnclubmcr, not @thelawnclub. Both accounts exist, but Instagram’s own metadata on the reel you sent says @lawnclubmcr posted it. That is the live one.
  • @boxpark is the Shoreditch site, not a group account. The fan-park and crowd-reaction content everyone praises is Croydon and Wembley. Follow those two for sport nights.
  • Freight Island is @freightislandmcr. @escapetofreightisland returns a page with no profile on it.

No true local comparable exists in Leicester. The outdoor scene here is conventional bar terraces rather than yards or container parks, so Merchants Yard is effectively the only one of its type in the city. That is a positioning line, not a gap in the research.
